# LLM Applications, Fine-Tuning, Interpretability & Model Evaluation

## 🎯 Exam Essentials

### 1. Instruction Fine-Tuning

- **Concept:** Instruction fine-tuning further trains a pretrained model using examples of human-like instructions and desired responses. The goal is to improve how well the model follows instructions and generates appropriate responses.
    
- **Important distinction:** Fine-tuning starts from an already pretrained model; it is **additional training**, not training a foundation model from scratch.
    
- **Exam trigger:**
    
    > “Further train a pretrained model to better follow instructions or produce desired responses”  
    > → **Instruction fine-tuning**
    

---

### 2. Human Feedback and Model Alignment

- **Concept:** Fine-tuning with human feedback can help align model behavior with human preferences.
    
- **Important goals:** Improve **helpfulness, honesty, and harmlessness**.
    
- **Why it matters:** LLMs can produce toxic, offensive, discriminatory, dangerous, or otherwise undesirable completions.
    
- **Exam trigger:**
    
    > “Align model responses with human preferences”  
    > → **Human feedback / alignment**
    
- **⚠️ Accuracy note:** Fine-tuning or human feedback can **reduce** undesirable behavior and improve alignment, but it does **not guarantee** that hallucinations, toxicity, or harmful outputs will be completely eliminated.
    

---

### 3. LLM Hallucinations

- **Concept:** A hallucination occurs when an LLM produces information that is **incorrect or unsupported**, potentially while sounding confident and convincing.
    
- **Important distinction:** A response can be fluent and confident while still being factually wrong.
    
- **Best practice:** Important information generated by an LLM should be checked against an **authoritative source** before being relied upon.
    
- **Exam trigger:**
    
    > “The model confidently provides a plausible but incorrect answer”  
    > → **Hallucination**
    

---

### 4. Responsible AI and Harmful Completions

- **Concept:** LLMs can generate harmful completions, including offensive, discriminatory, toxic, or dangerous content.
    
- **Important principles from the transcript:**
    
    - **Helpfulness**
        
    - **Honesty**
        
    - **Harmlessness**
        
- **Why this can happen:** LLMs are trained on very large collections of text, including internet content that can contain undesirable language or information.
    
- **Exam trigger:**
    
    > “Reduce harmful or undesirable model behavior while aligning responses with human values”  
    > → **Responsible AI / alignment techniques**
    

---

### 5. Model Interpretability

- **Concept:** Interpretability refers to how easily humans can understand a model's predictions and the reasons behind them.
    
- **Important distinction:**
    
    - **Model performance:** How well the model predicts.
        
    - **Model interpretability:** How understandable the model's predictions are.
        
- **Tradeoff:** Simpler models are generally easier to interpret, while complex models can capture nonlinear relationships that simpler models may miss.
    
- **Exam trigger:**
    
    > “Understand why the model made a particular prediction”  
    > → **Interpretability**
    

---

### 6. Intrinsic Analysis

- **Concept:** Intrinsic analysis interprets a model based on its inherent structure and is particularly applicable to models with **low complexity or relatively simple relationships** between inputs and predictions.
    
- **Important distinction:** Interpretability comes from characteristics of the model itself rather than applying a separate interpretation method after training.
    
- **Exam trigger:**
    
    > “A relatively simple model whose prediction logic can be understood from its structure”  
    > → **Intrinsic analysis**
    

---

### 7. Post Hoc Analysis

- **Concept:** Post hoc analysis interprets a **trained model after the fact** using the model's inputs and output predictions.
    
- **Important capability:** Can be applied to more complex models, including **neural networks**.
    
- **Model agnostic:** The transcript describes these methods as often being model agnostic, meaning the interpretation technique does not necessarily depend on the specific internal structure of the model.
    
- **Exam trigger:**
    
    > “Explain a trained neural network's predictions without relying on its internal structure”  
    > → **Post hoc analysis**
    

---

### 8. Local vs Global Interpretability

- **Local analysis:** Focuses on a **single data point or prediction**.
    
    - Exam trigger: “Why did the model make this particular prediction?”
        
- **Global analysis:** Examines the **overall behavior of the model**.
    
    - Exam trigger: “How does the model generally behave across its predictions?”
        

---

### 9. Evaluating LLMs

- **Concept:** Evaluating LLMs is more challenging than evaluating many traditional ML models because generated language can be **non-deterministic and open-ended**.
    
- **Traditional ML:** When known outputs exist, metrics such as accuracy can compare predictions with expected results.
    
- **LLMs:** Multiple generated responses can differ in wording while conveying related meanings, making straightforward exact-match evaluation less suitable.
    
- **Exam trigger:**
    
    > “Evaluate the quality of generated natural-language output against reference text”  
    > → Consider an appropriate **language-generation evaluation metric**.
    

---

### 10. ROUGE

- **Concept:** **ROUGE (Recall-Oriented Understudy for Gisting Evaluation)** is primarily used to evaluate automatically generated **summaries** against reference summaries.
    
- **Exam trigger:**
    
    > “Compare an automatically generated summary with a human/reference summary”  
    > → **ROUGE**
    

---

### 11. BLEU

- **Concept:** **BLEU (Bilingual Evaluation Understudy)** is designed to evaluate **machine-translated text** by comparing it with human/reference translations.
    
- **Exam trigger:**
    
    > “Evaluate machine translation against a reference translation”  
    > → **BLEU**

## ⚔️ Important Comparisons

### Instruction Fine-Tuning vs Human Feedback

|Concept|Use when|Key distinction|
|---|---|---|
|**Instruction fine-tuning**|Improving a model's ability to follow instructions|Further trains the model using instruction/response examples|
|**Human feedback**|Aligning model behavior with human preferences|Uses human preferences/feedback to guide desired behavior|

---

### Intrinsic vs Post Hoc Analysis

|Analysis|Use when|Key distinction|
|---|---|---|
|**Intrinsic analysis**|Model has relatively simple/interpretable structure|Understanding comes from the model's inherent structure|
|**Post hoc analysis**|Need to interpret an already-trained model, including complex models|Interpretation is performed after training|
|**Post hoc + local**|Need to understand one prediction|Focuses on a specific data point|
|**Post hoc + global**|Need to understand general model behavior|Focuses on overall model behavior|

---

### ROUGE vs BLEU

|Metric|Use when|Key distinction|
|---|---|---|
|**ROUGE**|Evaluating generated summaries|Generated summary ↔ reference summary|
|**BLEU**|Evaluating machine translation|Machine translation ↔ reference translation|

---

### Performance vs Interpretability

|Concept|Core question|Key concern|
|---|---|---|
|**Performance**|“How well does the model predict?”|Prediction quality|
|**Interpretability**|“Why/how did the model make this prediction?”|Understanding model behavior|

## 🧠 Exam Traps

### Trap 1 — A confident answer must be correct ❌

**Correct:** An LLM can generate a highly fluent and confident **hallucination**. Confidence and fluency do not establish factual correctness.

---

### Trap 2 — Fine-tuning eliminates hallucinations ❌

**Correct:** Fine-tuning or human-feedback-based alignment can **reduce undesirable behavior**, but it does not guarantee that hallucinations disappear.

---

### Trap 3 — Interpretability and performance mean the same thing ❌

**Correct:**

- **Performance** → how well the model predicts.
    
- **Interpretability** → how understandable those predictions are.
    

---

### Trap 4 — Intrinsic analysis is the preferred method for complex neural networks ❌

**Correct:** The transcript associates intrinsic analysis with **lower-complexity/simple models**. Post hoc methods can be used with complex models such as neural networks.

---

### Trap 5 — Local analysis means understanding the model as a whole ❌

**Correct:**  
**Local → one prediction**  
**Global → overall model behavior**

---

### Trap 6 — ROUGE is primarily for machine translation ❌

**Correct:** **ROUGE → summarization.**

---

### Trap 7 — BLEU is primarily for summarization ❌

**Correct:** **BLEU → machine translation.**

---

### Trap 8 — Human feedback means humans manually answer every user query ❌

**Correct:** In this context, human feedback is used to **guide model training/alignment toward preferred behavior**.

---

### Trap 9 — A simple, highly interpretable model must always outperform a complex model ❌

**Correct:** Simpler models can be easier to interpret but may fail to capture **complex nonlinear interactions**.

---

### Trap 10 — Post hoc analysis only works for simple models ❌

**Correct:** Post hoc analysis can be applied to **complex trained models**, including neural networks.

### Question 1

A financial institution uses an LLM to provide explanations of its internal compliance policies. During evaluation, the model generates a detailed explanation of a policy that does not exist. The response is fluent, confident, and appears credible to employees who are unfamiliar with the policy.

Which issue should the organization identify as the primary concern?

A. The model has low interpretability because employees cannot inspect its internal decision process  
B. The model is hallucinating by generating unsupported information as though it were factual  
C. The model is underfitting because it cannot reproduce the organization's policy terminology  
D. The model has high variance because its predictions are difficult to explain globally

**Answer:** **B**

**Why:** The defining clue is **confident generation of incorrect/unsupported information**. This is a hallucination. Interpretability concerns understanding model behavior, not whether the generated information is factually correct.

---

### Question 2

An organization has trained a neural network to approve or reject loan applications. Regulators ask the organization to investigate **why one particular applicant received a rejection**. The team does not need an explanation of the model's behavior across all applicants.

Which approach best matches the requirement?

A. Global intrinsic analysis  
B. Local post hoc analysis  
C. Global post hoc analysis  
D. Intrinsic analysis of the training dataset

**Answer:** **B**

**Why:** The requirement concerns **one specific prediction**, so the analysis is local. Because the model is already trained and is a complex neural network, post hoc analysis is appropriate.

### Question 3

A company develops an LLM-powered customer-support assistant. The company wants the assistant to respond according to preferred human behavior and reduce responses containing offensive or harmful content. It has access to human evaluations indicating which responses are preferable.

Which approach most directly addresses this objective?

A. Use human feedback to improve alignment with desired behavior  
B. Replace the pretrained model with a deterministic classification algorithm  
C. Increase the model's context window so every response contains more tokens  
D. Use a larger validation dataset and calculate traditional classification accuracy

**Answer:** **A**

**Why:** Human feedback can be used to **align model behavior with human preferences** and improve helpfulness, honesty, and harmlessness.

### Question 4

A machine translation company generates English translations from Japanese text and wants an automated metric that compares the generated translations with human-created reference translations.

Which metric is most appropriate?

A. ROUGE  
B. BLEU  
C. Global interpretability  
D. Classification accuracy

**Answer:** **B**

**Why:** **BLEU** is primarily designed for evaluating machine-translated text against reference translations. ROUGE is primarily associated with summarization.

### Question 7

An organization starts with a pretrained language model. What is the primary purpose of this training?
