
## 🎯 Exam Essentials

### 1. Selecting a Generative AI Model

- **Concept:** Choosing a generative AI model should be based on the project's **model type, performance requirements, capabilities, constraints, and compliance requirements**.
    
- **Important distinction:** There is no single foundation model that is optimal for every generative AI workload. Different models specialize in different types of content.
    
- Foundation models can support generation of:
    
    - Text and chat
        
    - Images
        
    - Code
        
    - Video
        
    - Embeddings
        
- **Exam trigger:**
    
    > “Select a foundation model based on required output type, capabilities, constraints, and business requirements”  
    > → Evaluate the **model's capabilities against the application's requirements**.

### 2. Generative AI Model Architectures

The transcript identifies several commonly used generative model types:

- **Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)**
    
- **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)**
    
- **Autoregressive models**
    

Each architecture has different advantages and disadvantages depending on the **type, complexity, and desired quality of the generated data**.

- **Exam trigger:**
    
    > “Choose an architecture for generating a particular type of data”  
    > → Consider the architecture's **capabilities and suitability for the data/task**, rather than assuming one architecture is universally best.

### 3. Foundation Models

- **Concept:** Foundation models (FMs) are large models trained on broad datasets that serve as a **baseline starting point** for developing generative AI applications.
    
- They can be adapted for specific domains and tasks.
    
- Different FMs specialize in different capabilities, such as:
    
    - Language understanding and generation
        
    - Conversational applications
        
    - Image generation
        
- **Important distinction:** A foundation model is generally a **starting point** that can be adapted to application-specific requirements rather than being limited to one narrowly defined task.
    
- **Exam trigger:**
    
    > “Large pretrained model used as the starting point for a generative AI application”  
    > → **Foundation model**
    

---

### 4. Foundation Models vs Traditional ML Models

- Foundation models are trained on **large, broad datasets** and can support multiple types of generative tasks.
    
- Traditional ML models are often developed for **more specific functions**.
    
- Different foundation models specialize in different areas, so model selection should be based on the application's requirements.

### Question 1

A retailer has deployed a generative AI assistant that creates product recommendations. The model produces grammatically correct responses, but customers frequently report that the recommendations do not address what they actually asked for.

The company wants to measure this particular aspect of generated-response quality.
